DairyTwin: a minimum-viable, explainable digital twin for dairy cows on commercially available farm data

Abstract · MSCA-PF 2026 proposal

European dairy is among the most data-rich and most decision-poor sectors of agriculture. A modern cow generates thousands of digital events per day, yet many small and medium farmers still decide which cow to look at first using memory and end-of-month spreadsheets. Here we present DairyTwin, a minimum-viable, explainable digital twin that runs on data European farms already export every day — parlour and AMS logs, milk recording, management events, activity–rumination collars, and weather.

Each cow is represented as a latent state vector with four components — production, perturbation, fertility, risk trajectory — updated daily by a hybrid probabilistic / machine-learning estimator. A constrained herd-prioritisation layer turns the cow-level state into a ranked daily worklist under explicit operator-time and treatment-budget constraints. Every recommendation is explained in a single sentence against the cow's own baseline, with calibrated uncertainty banding. The fellowship will validate the prototype retrospectively on four open datasets (Zenodo, Agroscope, INRAE, Teagasc) and release an MIT-licensed reference implementation jointly with ScaDS.AI Dresden/Leipzig.

1 · Excellence — research and innovation

The literature on precision dairy is strong in isolated cow-level monitoring[1,3] and moderately strong in cow-level risk models[2,4], but weak in the integration of latent-state estimation, herd-level prioritisation under realistic resource constraints, and farmer-facing explanation into a single, reproducible, openly licensed workflow. The MSCA-PF fellowship at TU Dresden's ScaDS.AI is positioned to close exactly this gap.

Specific objectives

  1. O1. Define a minimum-viable digital-twin schema for a single cow and a herd, with formal state-space semantics, over commercially available data.
  2. O2. Develop a hybrid state estimator (probabilistic state-space layer + ML residual layer) that outperforms one-shot baselines on lead time and precision-at-top-k, not on AUROC alone.
  3. O3. Build a constrained herd-prioritisation policy (integer / greedy / contextual-bandit variants) under explicit operator constraints.
  4. O4. Deliver a farmer-facing explainability layer based on cow-own-baseline reason codes, counterfactual sentences, and uncertainty banding.
  5. O5. Release an MIT-licensed reference implementation with a reproducible benchmark over Zenodo, Agroscope, INRAE, and Teagasc datasets.

2 · Methods

Cow-state representation

Each cow is represented by a latent state st = (sprodt, spertt, sfertt, sriskt), updated daily from a multi-source observation ot = (milk, AMS, collar, weather, events). A probabilistic state-space layer (Bayesian filter / HMM / MDP) produces calibrated daily scores and uncertainty bands; an ML residual layer (gradient boosting / penalised GLM) captures non-linear deviations the state-space layer does not.

Three evaluated architectures

ArchitectureMinimum inputsEvaluation focus
Simulation-only twin public datasets + literature parameters calibration, stability, sensitivity
Hybrid twin (primary) per-milking yield, parity, DIM, calvings, inseminations, treatments, activity, rumination, weather AUROC / PR-AUC, calibration, lead time, alert burden, precision@top-k
Farm-deployable prototype nightly farm export + management records + weather; collars optional prospective utility, time saved, false-alert rate

Explainability layer

  • SHAP over tree-based models for local and global attribution.
  • Reason codes against the cow's own baseline, not the herd: "rumination −18% vs 7-day mean", "rear-quarter milk flow atypically low", "two extra refusals".
  • Counterfactual sentences: "if rumination were within band and milking interval shorter, this cow would drop out of today's top 20".
  • Uncertainty banding: every score is rendered with a screen now / watch / no action category to protect against alert fatigue.

5 · Impact

The EU produces approximately 155 Mt of cow milk per year from about 20 million dairy cows on more than 1.4 million farms. Subclinical mastitis alone is estimated to cost the EU dairy sector on the order of €1.5 billion / year. A conservative 5% reduction in unnecessary inspections and antimicrobial treatments enabled by better daily prioritisation would represent an EU-level saving on the order of €75–100 M / year and a measurable contribution to the Farm-to-Fork and antimicrobial-stewardship targets — without removing the human from the decision.
